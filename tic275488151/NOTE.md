Long version of the ExoFOP note on TIC 275488151.01 (2026-11-02).
Candidate attribution, unrefereed.
Short version posted on ExoFOP.

TIC 275488151.01 (Montalto 2022) may be a nearby eclipsing binary. KIC 10014830 (TIC 275488133), a known Kepler EB with P = 3.0305267 d (Villanova Kepler EB Catalog: Prsa et al. 2011, Slawson et al. 2011, Kirk et al. 2016), is 83" (4 TESS pixels) from the target and 2.6 mag fainter (Tmag 13.8 vs 11.2). Its Kepler primary is about 43% deep (mean over the central +/- 0.126 d, our measurement) and about 11 h wide (catalogue), against the 0.210 d (5 h) CTOI duration. ExoFOP lists that 0.21 in hours, but the DIAmante value is in days.

Kepler shows no comparable signal on the target, KIC 10014894, at the EB ephemeris in 2009-2013. The EB is outside the target's optimal aperture in all 18 quarters. Stacked at the EB ephemeris, the target's PDCSAP gives 0.0 +/- 2.6 ppm over 424 events (nominal one-sided Gaussian 99.7% limit 7 ppm). With the TIC contamination ratio of 0.56, an on-target signal of comparable depth would be about 873 ppm in Kepler. 873 ppm box injections at the EB period, at 200 random phases away from its eclipses, are all detected, and 200 uninjected control phases give limits of 6-12 ppm (median 8). The target also has no KOI and no DR25 TCE, which argues against a persistent on-target signal at the CTOI period.

Why this EB, found post hoc after a planned 0.1% period match had rejected the pair: the CTOI period is 0.165% (4.2 sigma) shorter than the EB's, which counts against a pure leak unless DIAmante's period error is underestimated. The EB's own sector 14-15 eclipses, measured in an EB-centred TESS aperture, come about 0.038 d before its Kepler ephemeris; their diluted depth there is about 1.2 times the Kepler depth. Across sectors 14-15 the CTOI ephemeris runs from about 0.9 h after those eclipses to 1.3 h before them (+0.3 h and -0.8 h at the sector midpoints); that steady drift is the period mismatch. A PRF model with all 654 field stars predicts a leaked depth of about 325 ppm, averaged over +/- 0.105 d, in a circular aperture of radius 2 px. That is about 58% of the 558 ppm catalogue transit depth, though not like-for-like, since the two come from different estimators; the model does not reproduce DIAmante's extraction, and its uncertainty is not propagated. In DIAmante's spline-normalized LC2 the EB's secondary shows at radius 4 (2538 +/- 350 ppm against 2235 predicted). That shows EB light in the photometry, not that the EB causes the radius-2 dip.

What isn't settled: our TESS pixel fits failed their injection gates and did not locate the source. In radius 2, LC2 prefers a 5 h box at the CTOI ephemeris over the EB's eclipse shape; none of 200 EB injections went as far toward the box, but they were added after DIAmante's spline, so they do not include its effect. The EB template's fitted amplitude there is 1.2 +/- 0.4 times the predicted leak. DIAmante's LC1 (eigen-light-curve corrected) after our own detrending prefers the EB shape, but at about 3 times the predicted leak, and with the EB template included it still leaves a 326 +/- 110 ppm box (about 3 sigma; this joint fit has no injection calibration) at the CTOI timing. Neither single-model comparison passed its calibration. An illustrative spline test shows how spline normalization can weaken an 11 h eclipse, but not that it explains LC2's preference. TESS systematics near 3 d were not tested. The DV's centroid ranks favour the target for radii 1-3, including the selected radius-2 aperture, which counts against a leak; radius 4 selects another source. About 36% of a standard aperture's light comes from other stars, so other sources aren't excluded. Our own photometry of eight later sectors (40-82; two fall off the science array) was too noisy to decide. The CTOI ephemeris has expired by sector 40 (about +/- 0.3 d), so later sectors cannot be phased on it; they can test whether the dip follows the EB's eclipse times, re-measured in each sector since the catalogue flags timing and depth variations. I think KIC 10014830 is the most likely source, but the attribution is unresolved. No FPP computed. Thanks to M. Montalto for the public DIAmante light curve and DV report on ExoFOP.
